01

The verdict

Bottom line

Nothing in the AXIOM catalogue treats any of the five findings in this report — and four of the eight product categories are actively contraindicated by them.

The report's own framing is the correct one: the two headline findings are diagnostic problems, not optimisation problems. A fully suppressed TSH of 0.057 with an unresolved medication history, and two raised inflammatory markers thirteen days apart with no identified source, are questions for an endocrinologist and a dermatologist. Layering a GH secretagogue, an incretin agonist or an immunomodulator on top of that does not improve the picture — it destroys the signal the clinicians are using to read it. One compound (tirzepatide) has a genuine evidence base for a problem she actually has. It is still gated, and it should not come from a research-grade pen.

The five findings that constrain everything

Each finding below rules out a specific block of the catalogue. This is the whole logic of the assessment — the rest of the document is bookkeeping against these five lines.

Suppressed TSH, T4-dominant shape
TSH 0.057 · fT4 17.2 (81st pct) · fT3 3.5 (31st pct)
Biochemical thyrotoxicosis of exogenous shape. Until the medication list settles whether this is levothyroxine over-replacement or deliberate suppression after thyroidectomy for thyroid cancer, no compound that raises IGF-1 or carries a thyroid C-cell warning can be supplied. This single unknown removes the entire GH secretagogue category and gates the GLP-1s.
Unexplained inflammation, two draws
ESR 41 (≤20) · hs-CRP 6.20 (<3) · 13 days apart
Source not identified; dermatology workup ongoing. ESR and CRP are the only instruments the clinicians have to track it. Any immunomodulator — Thymosin α-1, LL-37, KPV, KLOW — moves those markers for reasons unrelated to the disease, and the next repeat test becomes uninterpretable. Cost of that is a missed diagnosis, not a wasted vial.
Prediabetes
HbA1c 6.0% · random glucose 126 / 118 mg/dL
The one finding with a plausible peptide target — and simultaneously the reason GH secretagogues are excluded. Growth hormone and GHRH analogues reduce insulin sensitivity and raise fasting glucose; tesamorelin in particular showed glycaemic worsening in a subset of trial participants. Wrong direction for a 6.0% A1c.
Mildly reduced eGFR
eGFR 71 · creatinine 0.9 · urine ACR not done
Ambiguous at 64 — normal ageing or early disease, undistinguished without ACR. It makes anything with GI-volume-loss potential (incretin agonists) or renal clearance sensitivity (injectable L-carnitine) a physician's call, not a catalogue call.
Microcytic, hypochromic red cells
MCV 80.3 · MCH 26.1 · Hb 13.7 · Mentzer 15.3
Iron deficiency vs thalassaemia trait, unresolved without ferritin and transferrin saturation. No peptide on the list addresses either. Listed here only because it is the fifth open question and reinforces the sequencing point: three cheap blood tests outrank the entire catalogue.
Active dermatology workup
Referring clinician Sp.KK · total IgE 21.6 · vit D 54.8
Not a lab abnormality, but the strongest single context clue in the report — and it rules out two compounds directly. LL-37 (cathelicidin) is an established autoantigen in psoriasis and is implicated in rosacea pathogenesis. Melanocortin agonists (PT-141) drive hyperpigmentation. Neither belongs anywhere near an undiagnosed skin presentation.

04

The gate — what must be true before tirzepatide is even a conversation

One compound survived to conditional. These are the conditions, in order. All five must clear, and the first two are questions no catalogue can answer.

  1. The thyroid history is known and documented. Is she on levothyroxine, and is the suppression intentional? If TSH is being held down deliberately after thyroidectomy for thyroid carcinoma, that history has to be on the table before any GLP-1/GIP agonist with a thyroid C-cell warning is considered. This is one question to the endocrinologist and it costs nothing.
  2. The inflammation has a name. ESR 41 and hs-CRP 6.20 with no identified source is an open diagnostic problem. Starting a compound that causes GI symptoms and weight change while that is unresolved makes the clinical picture harder to read at exactly the wrong moment.
  3. There is an actual weight indication. The report contains no weight, height or BMI. An HbA1c of 6.0 alone does not establish the case; without an anthropometric indication, the intervention is unjustified regardless of how good the trial data is.
  4. Renal status is characterised. Urine albumin-creatinine ratio, not repeated eGFR. An eGFR of 71 at 64 is ambiguous; ACR distinguishes normal ageing from early disease and changes the tolerance for a compound with volume-loss potential.
  5. It is prescribed, and it is pharmaceutical. If gates 1–4 clear, the endocrinologist prescribes BPOM-registered tirzepatide through a licensed pharmacy channel — with titration, monitoring and a named clinician. A research-grade pen is not a substitute for that in a 64-year-old with four unresolved findings, and AXIOM should not position it as one.
Note on the commercial read. This subject profile — 64, unexplained systemic inflammation, an unresolved possible oncological history, mildly reduced renal function — is the customer AXIOM should decline and refer, not convert. The downside case is not a refund; it is a serious adverse event in a patient whose diagnosis was still open, attributable to a research-use-only product. Referring cleanly costs one sale. The alternative costs the licence to operate.
